How I built GoldenDose · 2

Steps 1 to 3 · Plan

First, understand the people

Before writing any code, I decided who the app is for and what it must do.

Step 1

Requirements

I wrote down the problem, and who has it: Kamala, who takes many pills, and Priya, her daughter. Then I listed what the app must do.

Like telling the architect how many rooms you need.

Step 2

UX design

I planned every journey, step by step: setting up a medicine, the alarm ringing, tapping "Taken", the family getting a message.

Like planning how people walk through the house.

Step 3

Look and prototype

I chose big text, strong colours and huge buttons. Then I made a clickable model to try the idea before building the real thing.

Like a small model of the house, before laying bricks.

How I built GoldenDose · 3

Steps 4 and 5 · Foundation

How an app works: like a restaurant

Every app has four parts. Steps 4 and 5 planned how they fit together.

Frontend

Dining room

The screens Kamala and Priya see and tap.

API

Waiter

Carries requests to the kitchen and brings back answers.

Backend

Kitchen

Does the real work: checks, rules and alarm timing.

Database

Store room

Keeps everything safe: medicines, times, who took what.

Step 4 · Data design

I listed what the app must remember, such as people, medicines, doses and alerts, and how they connect. For example, one parent can have many carers.

Step 5 · API design

I wrote the waiter's "menu": every request the screens can make, like "show today's medicines" or "mark this dose as taken".

How I built GoldenDose · 4

Steps 6 and 7 · Build

Then I built the real app

With the plan ready, I built the kitchen first, then the dining room.

Step 6 · Backend and database

The hidden engine

It saves medicines and doses safely, keeps passwords secret, and checks every minute for missed doses. If a dose is 30 minutes late, it tells the family.

Step 7 · Frontend

Two apps people use

Kamala's app: one screen, a loud full-screen alarm, a pill picture, and the name read aloud. It works without internet.

Priya's family app: add medicines and photos, see today's doses, and choose how to get alerts.

The computer checks the app for me, like a robot clicking every button. It found real problems, and I fixed them.
